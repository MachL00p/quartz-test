# Harvesting moss
At the end of the rainy season, go for walks in the forest and harvest any type of moss you find. Cultivating moss for a week or two in closed environments is useful to see how they'll evolve in said environment, capital point to decide which type of moss is the best. Pick up the moss in clumps with the soil that's connected to them. Place the collected moss in closed freezer bags in which it can survive for a day or two, as long as the bag is closed.
Moss that has gone to seed is to be avoided: it tends to die in terrariums. If the moss in the terrarium starts growing little stalks, remove it.

# Preparing moss
Transfer the moss clumps from the freezer bags to containers that are at least 5 to 6 centimeters tall (without breaking the clumps too much), shallower containers tend to limit the moss's growth and kill it. If the moss is dry when placed in the container, spray it with water without over watering it so that the moss doesn't rot (it's better to underwater the moss than over watering it).
Once the moss is watered, put the lid of the container on and check on the moss every few days, pulling every piece that seems brown or mouldy. Moss that turns brown is moss that isn't suitable for closed environments.

# Making a jar terrarium
## Resources
    - Jar and lid
    - Pebbles/Gravel
    - Activated charcoal
    - Gauze
    - Soil
    - Moss
    - Sticks or long tweezers

Build the terrarium by layers:
 1. Pebbles/Gravel
 2. Charcoal
 3. Netting
 4. Moss and soil
 5. Decoration and seeds.
Fill the jar with about 2-3cm of gravel, sprinkle charcoal (about two tablespoons for a 7cm radius jar). Cut the netting to size, it should not be seen from the side of the jar. Wet the netting to prevent the thin layer of soil from falling through. The soil helps to even out the moss height. Add the moss as you like it. Add some plants and seeds if wanted.
Humidify the jar when done. If you're afraid that too much moisture was added, keeping the lid open a day or so will reduce the amount of humidity.
When filling a terrarium, keep the 1/3 filled 2/3 empty ratio in mind, this allows the terrarium to have enough space to survive.

# Display and care
It's generally unnecessary to water the terrarium if the soil looks humid and the walls are misting up. Depending on the ambient humidity, the necessary watering frequency could fluctuate. Placing the jar in a well lit area with no direct sunlight is necessary and works best for your plants. Rotating the jar helps the even growth of the plants.
If mould appears in the terrarium, take off the lid and wash it, then place the jar (with the lid still off) in the direct sunlight to attempt to kill the mould. At the end of the treatment day, water and replace the lid.
